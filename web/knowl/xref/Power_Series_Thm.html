Theorem 4.4.7.

Suppose that the function \(f(z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n(z-\alpha )^n\) has radius of convergence \(\rho >0\text{.}\) Then
  1. \(f\) is infinitely differentiable for all \(z\in D_{\rho}(\alpha )\text{.}\) In fact
    🔗
    🔗
  2. for all \(k\text{,}\) \(f^{(k)}(z)=\sum\limits_{n=k}^{\infty}n(n-1) \cdots (n-k+1)c_n(z-\alpha )^{n-k}\text{;}\) and
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(c_k=\frac{f^{(k)}(\alpha )}{k!}\text{,}\) where \(f^{(k)}\) denotes the \(k\)th derivative of \(f\text{.}\) (When \(k=0\text{,}\) \(f^{(k)}\) denotes the function \(f\) itself so that \(f^{(0)}(z) =f(z)\) for all \(z\text{.}\))
