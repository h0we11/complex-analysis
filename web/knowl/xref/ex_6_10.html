Example 6.2.16.

Show that
\begin{equation*} \int_{C_1}\overline{z}\,dz = -\pi i, \text{ but that } \int_{C_2}\overline{z}\,dz = -4i\text{,} \end{equation*}
where \(C_1\) is the semicircular path from \(-1\) to 1 and \(C_2\) is the polygonal path from \(-1\) to 1, respectively, shown in Figure 6.2.17.
🔗
Solution.
We parametrize the semicircle \(C_1\) as
\begin{align*} C_1 : z(t) \amp = -\cos t+i\sin t, \qquad \text{and}\\ dz \amp = (\sin t+i\cos t)\,dt, \quad \, \text{for} \quad 0 \le t \le \pi\text{.} \end{align*}
Applying Theorem 6.2.11, we have \(f(z)=\overline{z}\text{,}\) so
\begin{align*} f\big(z(t)\big) \amp = \overline{z(t)}=\overline{(-\cos t+i\sin t)}=-\cos t-i\sin t, \quad \text{and}\\ \int_{C_1}\overline{z}\,dz \amp = \int_0^{\pi}(-\cos t-i\sin t) (\sin t+i\cos t)\,dt\\ \amp = -i\int_0^{\pi}(\cos ^2t+\sin ^2t)\,dt = -\pi i\text{.} \end{align*}
🔗
We parametrize \(C_2\) in three parts, one for each line segment:
\begin{align*} \amp z_1(t) = -1+it, \quad dz_1 = i\,dt, \quad \text{and} \quad f\big(z_1(t)\big)=-1-it;\\ \amp z_2(t) = -1+2t+i, \quad dz_2 = 2\,dt, \quad \text{and} \quad f\big(z_2(t)\big)=-1+2t-i;\\ \amp z_3(t) = 1+i(1-t), \quad dz_3 = -i\,dt, \quad \text{and} \quad f\big(z_3(t)\big)=1-i(1-t)\text{,} \end{align*}
where \(0 \le t \le 1\) in each case. We get our answer by adding the three integrals along the three segments:
\begin{align*} \int_{C_2}\overline{z}\, dz \amp = \int_0^1(-1-it) i\,dt+\int_0^1(-1+2t-i) 2\,dt\\ \amp + \int_0^1[1-i(1-t)](-i)\,dt\text{.} \end{align*}
Separating the right side of this equation into its real and imaginary parts gives
\begin{equation*} \int_{C_2}\overline{z}\,dz=\int_0^1(6t-3)\,dt+i\int_0^1(-4)\,dt=-4i\text{.} \end{equation*}
Note that the value of the contour integral along \(C_1\) is not the same as the value of the contour integral along \(C_2\text{,}\) although both integrals have the same initial and terminal points.
